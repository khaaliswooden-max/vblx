02The gap — measured, not assertedScored against a committed 37-case benchmark
Spreadsheets
Broad but unguarded

Touch every document, but passively accept whatever is typed. No engine to catch what's wrong — structurally incapable of every coherence, genealogy and provenance case, regardless of operator skill.

Point solutions
Deep but narrow

Nail one task — SPC statistics, say — and leave everything else back in the spreadsheet. Even granting perfect in-scope accuracy, they still can't cover most of the job.

The union no one covers
The architecture-level opening
  • Cross-document integrity
  • Quality-plan (PPAP/FMEA/control-plan) coherence
  • Recall genealogy
  • Tamper-evident records
  • Active guardrails on bad data
Covering that union is an architecture-level job — not a feature either incumbent can bolt on. A passive spreadsheet has no engine for coherence, no graph for genealogy, no signing for provenance. That is the opening SQ‑OS is built for.
03The offering — one system of record for the whole job
Compliance core

PPAP, APQP, FMEA, control plans and SPC — assembled, cross-checked and kept consistent, priced for single-digit-margin shops.

Active guardrails

Catches the errors a spreadsheet waves through — stale revisions, mis-used risk scores, out-of-spec capability claims.

Traceability spine

Part-level serialization and genealogy: turn a total recall into a targeted one, from raw lot to finished serial.

Battery-passport ready

The same data spine produces the EU digital battery passport every EV supplier needs by February 2027.

04Why believe it — define the bar, then measure the gap
Most compliance software asks you to trust a claim. We built a testable benchmark of what "correct" means for automotive compliance — grounded in the AIAG / IATF standards and the EU battery regulation — and committed it cryptographically before building the product, a direct guard against optimizing to a moving target. Every check traces to a published standard; where a number can't be verified, we don't publish one. The moat compounds: switching cost once we hold the quality record of record, plus a mandate stack that only grows.
