# SQ-OS — 30 / 60 / 90-Second Pitch

*Working name: SQ-OS (Supplier Compliance & Quality OS). Thesis-stage — no fabricated traction. Pitches the opportunity, the rigor, and the plan.*
*Timing at a natural ~150 words/minute. Bracketed cues are for you, not to be spoken.*

---

## 30 seconds — the hook (~78 words)

Every automotive supplier below the top tier has to prove quality compliance — PPAP packages, FMEAs, part traceability — or they don't get the purchase order. Today most of them do it in spreadsheets, which are wrong more often than they're right. Supplier margins have collapsed below five percent, and the EU's digital battery passport becomes mandatory in 2027. We're building the compliance operating system for that long tail — the boring, mandated, mission-critical layer nobody serves affordably.

**Land on:** *"the layer nobody serves affordably."*

---

## 60 seconds — the gap and the rigor (~155 words)

Every automotive supplier below the top tier has to prove quality compliance — PPAP, FMEAs, part traceability — or they don't get the purchase order, and today most of them do it in spreadsheets that are wrong more often than right. Margins have collapsed below five percent; the EU battery passport goes mandatory in 2027.

Here's the gap. The tools that exist are either broad but unguarded — spreadsheets that passively accept whatever you type — or deep but narrow — a point solution that does statistics and nothing else. Neither covers the whole job: cross-document integrity, quality-plan coherence, recall genealogy, tamper-evident records. That union is the opening, and it's an architecture-level gap the incumbents can't patch.

And we don't just assert it. We built a benchmark that defines the testable bar first, then measured how far the incumbents fall short. The margin squeeze creates the demand; the 2027 deadline sets the clock.

**Land on:** *"the demand… and the clock."*

---

## 90 seconds — the plan and the ask (~230 words)

Every automotive supplier below the top tier has to prove quality compliance — PPAP, FMEAs, part traceability — or they don't get the purchase order. Today most do it in spreadsheets that are wrong more often than right. Supplier margins have fallen below five percent, and the EU battery passport becomes mandatory in February 2027.

The gap is structural. Existing tools are either broad but unguarded — spreadsheets that accept whatever you type — or deep but narrow — a point solution that does statistics and nothing else. Neither covers the union: cross-document integrity, quality-plan coherence, recall genealogy, tamper-evident records. And we don't assert that gap — we built a benchmark that defines the testable bar, then measured how far the incumbents fall short.

Our wedge is the most painful, most universal artifact: the PPAP and FMEA paperwork every supplier is forced to produce. Once we're the system of record for that, we attach the data spine — part-level serialization and genealogy that turns a total recall into a targeted one, and doubles as the battery passport every EV supplier needs by 2027. The moat is switching cost plus a mandate stack that only grows.

What we're looking for now is design partners — mid-tier suppliers with acute, mandated pain — to prove the wedge and price it. The bar is defined, the gap is measured, the clock is running.

**Land on:** *"The bar is defined, the gap is measured, the clock is running."*

## Delivery notes

- **One idea per breath.** The three versions are nested — the 60 contains the 30, the 90 contains the 60 — so you can expand or contract live without losing the thread.
- **The three anchors, in order of importance:** (1) *no PPAP, no PO* — the mission-critical hook; (2) *neither incumbent covers the union* — the defensible gap; (3) *2027 battery passport* — the clock. If interrupted, make sure at least the hook and one of the other two land.
- **Do not claim customers, revenue, or a shipping product.** The credible assets are the benchmark and the measured gap — lead with rigor, not hype.
- **If asked "what's the catch?":** willingness-to-pay is what the design-partner phase measures next. Say so plainly; the honesty reads as strength.
- **Numbers to keep exact:** supplier EBIT ~4.7% (2024); >40% of the largest suppliers non-investment grade; battery passport mandatory 18 Feb 2027. Everything else can be paraphrased.
